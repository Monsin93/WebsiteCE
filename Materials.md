## Slide 6 — Evolution of Natural Product Drug Discovery

| Era | Period | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional empirical era | Before 1900 | Use of crude extracts and herbal mixtures based on observation, traditional knowledge, empirical use, and ethnopharmacology — empirical and experience-based model |
| Bioactivity-oriented discovery | 1900s–1970s | Extraction, purification, and chemical modification; bioassay-guided isolation; isolation and activity-guided fractionation |
| Target-based drug discovery | 1980–2000 | Focus on specific molecular targets; target screening, mechanistic studies, combinatorial chemistry, high-throughput screening — mechanism-driven discovery |
| AI-driven and data-integrated discovery | 2010–Present | Use of large datasets and multi-omics technologies; predictive AI/ML modeling and virtual screening — data-driven and AI-assisted discovery |

**References:**
- Wang Y. et al. (2024). New drug discovery and development from natural products. *Current Opinion in Green and Sustainable Chemistry* 45, 100172.
- Chaachouay N. et al. (2024). Plant-derived natural products: a source for drug discovery and development. *Biomolecules* 3, 11.

## Slide 7 — Evolution of Drug Discovery Platforms: From QSAR to Generative AI

1. **Classical SAR/QSAR** — Hand-picked molecular properties; linear statistical equations; chemist-driven design
2. **Machine Learning** — Learns patterns from data; decision-tree ensembles & support-vector machines; higher predictive accuracy
3. **Deep Learning** — Multi-layer neural networks; reads molecules as text sequences; large-scale pretraining
4. **Graph Neural Networks** — Molecules as graphs (atoms + bonds); message passing between atoms; topology-aware learning
5. **Generative AI** — Designs brand-new molecules; self-attention backbone; diffusion-based generation
